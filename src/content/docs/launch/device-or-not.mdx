You met the intended purpose in [Part 1](../../product/purpose/). Here is what it sets in motion. Two questions, in this order:

1. **Qualification:** is the software a medical device at all?
2. **Classification:** if it is, which class? The class decides who has to check it before it can be sold or given to patients.

## Qualification

Under the EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR), software is a medical device when its manufacturer intends it for a **medical purpose**: diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of a disease, among others. What counts is the stated purpose and what the software **does**:

| Usually not a device on its own | May make it a device |
|---|---|
| storing what a user typed | interpreting it (scores, risk levels) |
| showing it back, as entered | alerting someone based on it |
| simple search and sorting | recommending an action for a person's health |
| general wellbeing and lifestyle content | anything aimed at a specific disease |

The **client** is the manufacturer and makes the decision, normally with a regulatory expert. Your job is to give them the facts: the purpose in four lines, the list of functions, and what each function does with health data. In Poland, the competent authority for medical devices is URPL (*Urząd Rejestracji Produktów Leczniczych, Wyrobów Medycznych i Produktów Biobójczych*).

## Classification and route

If the software is a device, **Rule 11** in Annex VIII of the MDR sets its class:

- Software that gives information used to take decisions for diagnosis or therapy: **class IIa**, or **IIb** if those decisions could cause a serious deterioration of health or a surgical intervention, or **III** if they could cause death or irreversible deterioration.
- Software that monitors physiological processes: **IIa**, or **IIb** for vital physiological parameters where a change could mean immediate danger to the patient.
- All other software: **class I**.

<Predict id="narrow" question="The clinic would love a feature that suggests a breathing exercise whenever a participant reports feeling anxious. Recommending an action based on health data could push the app towards a device. What options does the client have?">

At least three. **Take the device route**, with its cost and time. **Narrow the feature** so it no longer reacts to health data, for example a breathing exercise that anyone can open at any time, the same for everyone. Or **move the decision to a person**: the study team reviews entries under its own clinical procedures, outside the app. Which one is right is the client's decision, made with an expert, but you should put all three on the table. This is the "device? → narrow the purpose" loop on the map.
