## What does it really cost?

The build is the visible part. The running costs are the part people forget: hosting, support, updates when Android and iOS change, store fees, and the client's own staff time. **Total cost of ownership** adds them up over the whole period of use, and **cost per participant per month** makes it comparable with the alternatives from [Part 1](../../product/options-and-recommendation/), including paper.

## A pilot, with the criteria fixed first

A pilot is a small, time-boxed launch that answers one question: **should we go ahead?** It only answers it honestly if the success criteria are agreed **before** anyone sees the results. Otherwise, any result can be read as a success.

```text title="docs/pilot.md (illustrative)"
Pilot       10 participants, 30 days, from 2026-12-01
Question    Should the next group of 50 use the app?
Go if       8 of 10 participants find the contact without help (short check-in call)
            version questions to support: fewer than 2 per week
Stop if     any personal-data incident
Not measured  long-term use; participants without a smartphone
Agreed by   Anna (coordinator), 2026-11-24
```

## Go, change, or no-go

The business case is one page: the decision, the total cost and the cost per participant per month, the pilot results against the criteria, the risks, and the alternatives. **"No" is a valid result**: if paper does the job for less, recommending paper is good engineering, not a failure.

<AtWork>A business case with a range, a pilot with criteria fixed in advance, and the courage to recommend "no": this is what clients and managers remember about a developer.</AtWork>

:::tip[Do this in your project]
Put your client's numbers into the calculator, and write down the source of each one. Draft the pilot for your brief: one question, two or three success criteria, one stop criterion, what you will not measure. Send it to your client for agreement before anything else.
:::
