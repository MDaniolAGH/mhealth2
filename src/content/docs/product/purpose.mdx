The **intended purpose** says what a product is for. Under the EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR), software intended for a medical purpose, such as diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, treatment or alleviation of a disease, is a **medical device**. A device needs a quality management system, risk management, a software life-cycle process (IEC 62304), clinical evaluation and a CE mark. That changes the cost of every later decision by an order of magnitude.

What counts is the **purpose the manufacturer states and the functions the software has**, not a label such as "research" or "for information only". Storing and displaying what a user typed usually does not make software a device. Interpreting data, scoring it, alerting on it or recommending an action may.

<Predict id="purpose-flip" question="The Mood Tracker stays exactly the same: same code, same screens. Only the sentence in the app store changes, from &quot;a mood diary for study participants&quot; to &quot;helps patients manage depression&quot;. Does anything change?">

Possibly everything. "Manage depression" states a purpose of treating or alleviating a disease. With that sentence, the same code may be medical-device software, with all the obligations that follow. The purpose is the product's legal identity, which is why only the client may state it, and why an agent must never write it.

</Predict>

## Four lines

Write the purpose in four lines, as the course skill `mh-intended-purpose` asks:

```text title="docs/product.md: intended purpose (illustrative)"
For whom      adult participants of the clinic's six-month study
Situation     at home, once a day, on their own phone
Action        record how they feel, and see their own entries
Consequence   none for their care: entries are not used for diagnosis or
              treatment; the study team reads them for research only
Quote         "A self-report mood diary for participants of the clinic's
              study. It is not used for diagnosis or treatment."
              (client log, 2026-11-12)
```

Then, for every feature or text you are about to add, write the same four lines and compare. If the **action** or the **consequence** moves towards diagnosis or treatment, stop and ask.

:::danger[Agents write medical claims without being asked]
Ask an agent for a store description, a README or onboarding text, and it will often add benefits: "helps you manage your mood", "supports your mental health". Each such sentence is a claim about the purpose. The Mood Tracker's `AGENTS.md` has the rule you need: *"Flag any change that could make the intended purpose in `docs/product.md` untrue, before writing code."* Apply it to text as well as to code.
:::
