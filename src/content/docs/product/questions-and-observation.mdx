## Questions that change the recommendation

Client time is scarce. Ask only questions whose answers change what you will propose. For each one, write down what you would do with each possible answer: if no answer would change anything, do not ask it.

```text title="docs/questions.md (illustrative)"
1. Must the purpose be stated inside the app, or is the consent form enough
   for the ethics committee?
   Why: the main assumption behind the whole request.
   If "in the app"  → an About screen (or the store text, if that counts).
   If "the form"    → a printed sheet; no software change at all.

2. Who answers participants' questions, and when?
   Why: nobody owns it yet; the screen cannot show a contact that does not exist.
   If a named person  → show the contact.
   If nobody          → show no contact, and tell the client this is a risk.
```

A good question is **open, neutral and specific**. It does not suggest the answer ("Would a nice About screen help?") and it asks about the client's world, not about technology they may not know.

:::note[Discovery synthesis with an agent]
After interviews, an agent can cluster your notes into themes in minutes. Insist that **every theme cites a quote**, and check that each quote exists and says what the theme claims. Interview notes are personal data, often health data: use anonymised notes, keep them out of consumer AI tools, and in this course use synthetic material only.
:::

## The client meeting

You have three questions, each with "if A → X, if B → Y". The meeting is where assumptions turn into facts, or into new questions.

Write down what the client **said**, not what you hoped, with the date and who said it. Then send it back for a check. Those lines are now the only source of client facts:

```text title="docs/client-log.md (excerpt, illustrative)"
2026-11-13  Meeting with Anna (coordinator) and Tomek (support)
- The committee requires the purpose to be stated in the app itself. (Anna)
- Contact for participants: not decided; Anna asks the head of the clinic.
- Wording may have to change during the study, without a new app release. (Anna)
- The version must be easy to read out over the phone. (Tomek)
```

Notice the third line. It will matter on the [options page](../options-and-recommendation/#options).

## Observations from the app

The prototype you took over is full of surprises. Report what it **does**, prove it, and say why it matters to this client. Keep three things apart:

- **Observation**: what the app or the code does, with evidence (`file:line`, or steps that anyone can repeat with synthetic data).
- **Interpretation**: what it might mean. Label the uncertain parts.
- **Implication**: why the client should care.

Do not start from "find the bug". The word *bug* is only right when the expected behaviour was agreed and the app contradicts it. An agent is good at the search; you reproduce what it found before it goes into your report.

Run `/ba-questions` and keep only the questions that pass the "if A → X, if B → Y" test, at most five. Then pick your client's main concern and run `/ba-app-observation` on it. Reproduce the agent's evidence yourself before anything goes into your dossier.
:::
