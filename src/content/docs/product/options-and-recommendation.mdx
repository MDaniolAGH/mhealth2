## Options, including the ones that are not software

The cheapest licence is rarely the cheapest option. Always compare at least:

- **keep as is, or simplify**;
- **change the way of working**, with little or no software change;
- **use an existing product or service**;
- **adapt the existing app**.

Compare them by **total cost of ownership**: the one-off work, the running cost, and the **client's own time and duties** for as long as the study lasts. A screen that needs an app release for every change of wording costs the client something every time.

<Predict id="cheapest" question="The clinic's need: participants know what the app is for and whom to contact, and support can tell which version a participant runs. Before you look at the board: which option do you expect to win?">

It depends on the client's **hard limits**, not on your preference. If the ethics committee insists on the app itself stating the purpose, only the screen survives. If the wording must be changeable within a week without a release, the screen is out. If both limits hold, nothing survives, and the honest recommendation is to go back to the client. Switch the limits on below and watch.

Three things to notice. First, the board did not choose: the client's limits did. Second, when no option survives, the useful answer is to say **which limit to relax**, not to pick the least bad option quietly. Third, every number on the board is an estimate, so the next question is how good the estimates are.

## Estimates: a range, not a number

A **three-point estimate** asks for an optimistic (O), a most likely (M) and a pessimistic (P) value, and combines them: **E = (O + 4M + P) / 6**, with a spread of about **(P − O) / 6**. Give the client the range, not the single number. Change the values below and watch the expected value move with the pessimistic tail.

<Estimator task="Option C, the About screen, in team hours: build, tests, review, documentation" o={6} m={10} p={20} />

Agents speed up writing code. They do not speed up asking the client, checking the agent's work, reviewing, or regulatory work, and they add some new work of their own. So with agents, estimates should get **wider, not narrower**. An agent can draft O, M and P for each task; treat them as hypotheses. In W6 you compare them with the *Time* lines in your agent log, which is how estimates get better.

:::note[Test a risky assumption with a throwaway prototype]
The assumption with the biggest impact is often "people will use it". You can test it in a day or two: a clickable prototype, made with a prototyping tool (such as v0, Lovable or Bolt) or a quick Flutter mock-up, shown to three to five people. Report what you learned **and what you did not test**. The prototype is thrown away afterwards: it has no architecture, no gate and no SOUP register, so it is never the starting point for the real code. Use synthetic data only, and no real patients.
:::

## The one-page recommendation

The client should be able to decide after reading one page. The course skill `ba-recommendation` uses this structure:

1. **Recommendation**: one sentence that answers the decision.
2. **Why**: three to five reasons, each pointing to this client's evidence.
3. **Scope**: what is included, and what is explicitly not.
4. **Cost and responsibilities**: one-off, running, and what stays with the client.
5. **Risks**: the top three, and how each is handled.
6. **What would change this**: the assumptions it depends on, and the result that would make you stop (the **kill criterion**).
7. **Next step**: one concrete action, and who takes it.

```text title="docs/recommendation.md (excerpt, illustrative)"
Recommendation  Add an About screen to the app (option C), together with one
                sentence on the consent form (part of option B).
Why             The ethics committee asked for the app to state its purpose
                (client log 2026-11-12); support needs the version (same entry);
                C fits the sprint budget (E ≈ 11 h, range about 6–16 h).
What would      If the committee accepts the consent form alone, choose B and
change this     skip the software change. If the wording must change monthly,
                load the text from the server instead (a new decision).
Kill criterion  If the client cannot name a contact before release, the screen
                ships without one, and we tell the client this is a risk.
Next step       Coordinator asks the committee question 1 by Friday.
```

An agent drafts this page well. Make it work for you, not just with you:

```text title="Prompts (Copilot)"
Argue for the option I rejected, as convincingly as you can. Use only
evidence from docs/ and the client log.

List the facts that would change this recommendation, and for each, the
cheapest way to find out. Label every number as SOURCED (with source and
date) or ESTIMATE (with its basis). Mark anything else [UNVERIFIED].
```

The first prompt protects you from an agent that agrees with whatever you seem to prefer. The second keeps invented numbers, such as prices, market sizes and statistics, out of a document your client will act on.

Once the client decides, the recommendation becomes **issue #7**, and the About screen enters the [AI SDLC at stage 1](../../ai-sdlc/idea-to-plan/#stage-idea).
