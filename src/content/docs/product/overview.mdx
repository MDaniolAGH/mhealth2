An agent will write a fluent analysis, twenty user stories and a cost table in a few minutes, and it writes them just as fluently when they are wrong. The value moves to the person who can tell the difference.

This part is the business side of the AI SDLC: everything **before** stage 1, from a client's message to the request that becomes issue #7.

## Who does what?

<Predict id="sort-before" question="Before the sorting exercise: which of the four do you think does the <b>least</b> in product work with agents: the agent, you, the client, or &quot;nobody yet&quot;?">

There is no single right answer, but most people underestimate **"nobody yet"**. In real projects many decisions have no owner at first: who answers participants' questions, who approves the wording. Writing them down as open questions is part of the job. An agent never does it on its own: it fills the gap with a plausible answer.

</Predict>

## Where you use this in the course

Nothing is handed in week by week. What you practise in each class goes into the final project, which you submit at the end of the course.

| When | Product stage | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **W2** | Understand and choose: the prototype you took over | due diligence in three dimensions (technical, regulatory, business); the value proposition; options including doing nothing; three-point estimates |
| **W3** | From intended purpose to requirements | the purpose and what it costs; requirements and priorities; a throwaway prototype to test a need |
| **W4** | Building and checking | a bounded engineering contribution: a change, its evidence, its cost |
| **W5** | Handover and the route to market | regulation if it is a medical device, data protection if it is not; running and maintenance costs |
| **W6** | Business case | total cost of ownership, a pilot with success criteria agreed in advance; "no" as a valid result |

<AtWork>Product managers and analysts use agents for first drafts every day. The scarce skill is judgement: knowing which line of a draft is evidence and which is a guess.</AtWork>

:::
