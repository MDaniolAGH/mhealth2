Writing code is getting cheaper every month. Deciding **what** to build, **for whom**, and **on what evidence** is not. An agent will happily write a fluent analysis, twenty user stories and a cost table in a few minutes, and it will write them just as fluently when they are wrong. The value moves to the person who can tell the difference.

This part of the guide is the business side of the AI SDLC: the work **before** stage 1, from a client's brief to the request that becomes issue #7. It uses the same fictional client, the rehabilitation clinic from W1, and the course's own business-analysis skills (`/ba-*`), which you run in Copilot.

## Who does what?

<Predict id="sort-before" question="Before the sorting exercise: which of the four do you think does the <b>least</b> in product work with agents: the agent, you, the client, or &quot;nobody yet&quot;?">

There is no single right answer, but most people underestimate **"nobody yet"**. In real projects many decisions have no owner at first: who answers participants' questions, who approves the wording. Writing them down as open questions is part of the job. An agent never does it on its own: it fills the gap with a plausible answer instead.

</Predict>

## Where you use this in the course

| When | Product stage | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **W2** | Understand and choose: assessing the prototype you took over | due diligence in three dimensions (technical, regulatory, business); the value proposition; options including doing nothing; three-point estimates |
| **W3** | From intended purpose to requirements | the purpose and what it costs; requirements and priorities; a throwaway prototype to test a need |
| **W4** | Building and checking | a bounded engineering contribution: a change, its evidence, its cost |
| **W5** | Handover and the route to market | regulation if it is a medical device, data protection if it is not; running and maintenance costs |
| **W6** | Business case | total cost of ownership, a pilot with success criteria agreed in advance; "no" as a valid result |

:::tip[Do this in your project]
Run `/ba-analyst` on your brief in Copilot. Then sort its output with the four bins above: mark what the agent may keep, what you must check, what only your client can answer, and what nobody owns yet. Put the "nobody yet" items at the top of your questions for the next client meeting.
:::
