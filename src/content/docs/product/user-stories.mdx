A **user story** is a need the client agreed to, written from the point of view of the person who has it:

> **As a** *[who]*, **I want** *[what]*, **so that** *[why]*.

The course's `ba-requirements` skill writes the same thing as a requirement: *"[Who] can/must [do or get what], so that [goal]."* Use whichever your team prefers. Three rules matter more than the format:

1. **Need before solution.** "Participants know whom to contact" is a need. "A contact button in the app bar" is an idea about how to meet it. Ideas stay ideas until the client approves them.
2. **Every story has a source**: a line in the client log, a stakeholder, an observation. No source, no story: it goes on the *Ideas* list.
3. **Small.** A story you cannot finish and check in a few days is several stories (next page).

## The story is the agent's input

A good story with its criteria **is** the prompt for the AI SDLC: it becomes GOAL-1 and `docs/spec-about.md` (stage 3), and the agent plans from it. Teams that write specs for agents get better plans, because the agent has less to guess. Agents are also good critics of stories, if you ask them to be:

```text title="Prompts (Copilot)"
/ba-requirements docs/client-log.md
Write user stories only from docs/client-log.md. Cite the log line for each.
Put anything without a source under "Ideas (not approved)".

Act as a strict reviewer of docs/stories.md. For each story: does it name a
solution instead of a need? Is it testable? Is it two stories in one? Is the
source real? Do not rewrite the stories; list the problems.
```

Mostly plausible stories nobody asked for: features that "apps like this usually have", reasonable expectations, nice-to-haves. Some may be good ideas. None is a requirement until the client agrees. <AtWork>Stories with sources are how you avoid the classic “but I never asked for this” at the demo.</AtWork>

:::tip[Do this in your project]
Ask the agent for stories from your client log, with the citation rule above. Open every cited line and check that it says what the story claims. Move the rest to *Ideas*, and bring the Ideas list to your client as questions, not as a plan.
:::
